# Adjacent-framework crosswalk

AI Posture is positioned as an output maturity assertion. It does not replace governance frameworks, risk frameworks, compliance programs, certifications, audits, or legal advice.

## Governance management systems

Typical unit of analysis: organizational governance program.

Typical evidence type: policies, roles, procedures, risk registers, management reviews, internal controls.

How AI Posture differs: AI Posture measures externally inspectable output behavior across People, Infrastructure, and Regulation. A governance program may produce the evidence AI Posture needs, but the program is not itself the posture.

Useful relationship: governance systems can organize the work. AI Posture can report whether the work has produced defensible behavior.

## Risk frameworks

Typical unit of analysis: risks, harms, controls, impacts, likelihood, severity, treatment plans.

Typical evidence type: risk assessments, control mappings, mitigation plans, impact assessments, monitoring records.

How AI Posture differs: AI Posture is progressive maturity, not exposure scoring. New risk can reveal a bounded scope or an unmet obligation, but exposure alone does not erase mature behavior already evidenced.

Useful relationship: risk frameworks identify what must be managed. AI Posture reports the maturity of observable management output.

## Compliance and legal conformance programs

Typical unit of analysis: obligations in a statute, regulation, contract, or jurisdiction.

Typical evidence type: obligation registers, control mappings, interpretations, audit trails, certifications, legal review.

How AI Posture differs: Regulation is one vector, not the whole framework. A strong Regulation vector does not prove mature People or Infrastructure behavior.

Useful relationship: compliance programs can feed the Regulation vector. AI Posture prevents compliance maturity from being mistaken for whole-organization AI readiness.

## Capability maturity models

Typical unit of analysis: process maturity across staged levels.

Typical evidence type: process documentation, repeatability, measurement, optimization, governance cadence.

How AI Posture differs: AI Posture applies one shared level shape across multiple actor classes and aggregates by the minimum in-scope vector. It is not a process average.

Useful relationship: maturity-model language helps stakeholders understand progression. AI Posture adds cross-vector constraint logic and time-stamped assertions.

## Behavior-change and adoption models

Typical unit of analysis: human adoption, practice change, training, norms, incentives, behavior over time.

Typical evidence type: surveys, training records, usage logs, cohort analysis, qualitative observations.

How AI Posture differs: People is one vector. The framework rejects sentiment alone as maturity evidence and requires behavior that can be inspected while preserving privacy.

Useful relationship: behavior-change work can improve the People vector. AI Posture keeps that vector connected to infrastructure and regulatory constraints.

## Board, regulator, partner, and customer usefulness

Boards need a concise operating ceiling and the investment case. The constraining vector supplies both.

Regulators need evidence boundaries. AI Posture should show what is asserted, what is estimated, what is verified, and when it was stamped.

Partners and customers need a defensible signal without reading the full internal program. AI Posture can summarize the claim while preserving evidence paths.

Practitioners need next actions. The framework points to the vector that bounds the aggregate, not a generic improvement list.

Agents need parseable artifacts. The score, scope, vector levels, next-review date, evidence checklist, and estimate-not-verified label should remain machine-readable.

## Positioning guardrails

Do not claim AI Posture replaces named frameworks.

Do not imply certification, audit, legal advice, or compliance guarantee.

Do not treat reference implementations as required.

Do not average vector levels into a more flattering score.

Do not publish named third-party comparisons without verified sources and stable citations.
